# Venice: A Curious Traveler's Guide to the Lagoon City, Its History, Art and Daily Life

## Executive Summary

Venice is a city of about a thousand years of independent government, built on a group of islands in a shallow lagoon on Italy's northeastern coast (Wikipedia: Venice)(Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
The Republic of Venice existed from the 8th century until 1797, and the city was its capital for almost a millennium, from 810 to 1797 (Wikipedia: Venice).
Its power rested on the sea: it was a leading European trading power and the most successful of Italy's maritime republics (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice)(Wikipedia: Maritime republics).
Its trade in silk, grain and spice, and its navy, made it wealthy from the Middle Ages through the Renaissance (Wikipedia: Venice).
The decline began around 1500, when Portugal's sea route to the East and Ottoman wars eroded its trade and possessions (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice)(Wikipedia: Venice).
Napoleon conquered the city on 12 May 1797, and the Republic lost its independence (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
The city's artistic and musical legacy remains central to its appeal: Titian, Tintoretto and Veronese, and the composers Monteverdi, Vivaldi and Albinoni, are among its best-known names (Wikipedia: Venetian painting)(Wikipedia: Music of Venice)(Wikipedia: Venice).
The Venice Biennale, founded in 1893, still draws visitors to the Giardini and the wider city (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale).
Venice is fragile today: it faces acqua alta, subsidence and sea-level rise, with a likely rise of 21–52 cm by 2100 (Wikipedia: Sea level rise in Venice)(Wikipedia: Venice).
The MOSE barrier system, with gates at the three lagoon inlets, is designed to hold back tides up to 3 metres, but it has faced long delays and cost overruns (Wikipedia: MOSE).
For your visit, walk the city's bridges and calles and use the ACTV water buses, which run along the Grand Canal (Wikipedia: Actv).
Plan for the essential sights, St Mark's Basilica, the Doge's Palace, the Grand Canal and the Rialto Bridge, and make room for quieter stops such as the Scuola Grande di San Rocco (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica)(Wikipedia: Scuola Grande di San Rocco).
Visit the lagoon islands of Murano, Burano and Torcello, which are each a separate trip from the centre (Wikipedia: Murano)(Wikipedia: Burano)(Wikipedia: Torcello).
Before you go, check veneziaunica.it and comune.venezia.it for current practical details, including the day-trip access fee.

## From Lagoon Settlements to Maritime Republic, and Decline
The Republic of Venice was a sovereign state and maritime republic in northeast Italy that lasted about a millennium, from the 8th century until 1797 (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
Its traditional history starts with a founding on 25 March 421, said to be the work of authorities from Padua who set up a trading post (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
This is a later tradition: no surviving records deal directly with the founding, the church said to mark it dates no earlier than the eleventh century, and scholars do not accept the account (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
What the sources treat as better supported is that the Rialto islands were not fully urbanised until much later, from the 9th century (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
Tradition holds that the first population were refugees fleeing Hun and Germanic invasions from the mid-second to mid-fifth centuries (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
The earliest settlements were not on the Rialto: Olivolo (now San Pietro di Castello) was inhabited in the 5th century and was politically important (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
The story of Torcello, where a bishop is said to have been guided by a "voice from heaven" in 568, is a traditional account (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
By the late 7th century the lagoon communities had united for mutual defence as the Duchy of Venetia, with an early central committee of tribunes traditionally dated to c. 568 (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
Orso Ipato, elected early in the 8th century and confirmed by Byzantium, is historically attested as the first doge (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
The longer list of doges beginning in 697 with Paolo Lucio Anafesto rests on later chronicles that modern scholars do not accept (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
In 809 Pepin's siege of Malamocco lasted six months and failed, and Venice gained lasting independence in practice (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
Around 810 Agnello Participazio moved the ducal seat to the Rivo Alto group, the start of the urbanisation that formed the heart of Venice (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
In 1000 Doge Pietro II Orseolo led a fleet against the Narentine pirates and took the title "Dux Dalmatie", establishing Venice's prominence over the Adriatic (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
Venice grew rich in the High Middle Ages by controlling trade between Europe and the Levant, and joined the Crusades almost from the beginning (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
In 1204 the Fourth Crusade, diverted from its original course, sacked Constantinople, and Venice gained strategic territories in the Aegean, including Crete and Euboea (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
Rivalry with Genoa ran through the 13th and 14th centuries, with Venice winning at Curzola's rival battles and destroying the Genoese fleet at Chioggia in 1380 (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Maritime republics).
In the 15th century Venice built a Stato da Mar overseas and a Terraferma on the mainland, and by 1410 had taken most of Venetia, including Verona and Padua (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
By the end of the 15th century Venice had about 180,000 inhabitants and was probably the richest city in the world (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
Constantinople fell to the Ottomans on 29 May 1453, but Venice kept a colony there and in 1454 the Ottomans granted it ports and trading rights (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
The Ottoman wars of 1463–79 cost Venice Negroponte, and the 1479 peace required an annual tribute (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
The League of Cambrai (1508), led by Pope Julius II, aimed to divide Venetian lands, and on 14 May 1509 Venice was crushed at Agnadello (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
Venice recovered its mainland up to the Adda after seven years of war, but the sources treat 1509 as the end of Venetian expansion (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
The sources date the slow decline from around 1500, as Vasco da Gama's sea route to India in 1498 and rival ports such as Trieste, Ancona and Livorno drew trade away (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice).
The Cretan War (1645–69) ended with the loss of Crete, and the Morea was lost at the Treaty of Passarowitz in 1718 (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
In 1789 Ludovico Manin was elected the last doge, and by 1792 the merchant fleet had shrunk to 309 merchantmen (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).
In 1797 Napoleon declared war, the Maggior Consiglio voted to hand over power on 12 May, and the Treaty of Campoformio made Venice and its possessions Austrian on 18 October 1797 (Wikipedia: History of the Republic of Venice) (Wikipedia: Timeline of the Republic of Venice).

## How Geography Shaped Venice: Government, Economy, Architecture, Culture

The lagoon offered refugees protection in the fifth and sixth centuries and later sheltered the Venetian Republic's growth; Venice's sea-only connection with Ravenna encouraged increasing autonomy (Wikipedia: Venetian Lagoon) (Wikipedia: Venice).
Venice belonged to the maritime republics, states whose prosperity rested on navigation and trade, and whose governments generally expressed the interests of leading merchant families (Wikipedia: Maritime republics).
In Venice, government became the privilege of the merchant nobility from 1297 (Wikipedia: Maritime republics).

Protection did not mean disconnection: Venice traded with Byzantium and Asia, while its navy protected sea routes against piracy (Wikipedia: Venice).
Its mainland territories—the *terraferma*—provided a defensive buffer, secured Alpine trading routes and helped guarantee wheat supplies (Wikipedia: Venice).
Competition with Genoa repeatedly became war; during the War of Chioggia, Genoese forces besieged Venice before being forced to surrender (Wikipedia: Maritime republics).

Trade also carried artistic influences: sculptures, columns and capitals arrived from Byzantium to decorate merchants' homes, helping create a Venetian-Byzantine architecture of spacious loggias, rounded arches and multicoloured marble (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
Byzantine influence persisted in Venetian Renaissance art, while the Republic used artistic patronage as a political asset (Wikipedia: Venetian Renaissance).
Its cultural reach extended beyond the islands: mainland and Adriatic territories contributed painters and architects, and most major sixteenth-century Venetian painters were not city natives (Wikipedia: Venetian Renaissance).
Venice also became Italy's leading publishing centre, with Aldus Manutius prominent and Venetian editions circulating across Europe (Wikipedia: Venetian Renaissance).

Read the Grand Canal's *palazzi*, or palaces, as an interpretation of this union of commerce and display: noble families spent lavishly on them to advertise wealth and their bond with the lagoon (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
The practical connection is clearest in the *fondaco*, a combined warehouse and merchant's residence, whose waterside portico facilitated unloading (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
Look for the airy central façade and more solid sides: working arrangements helped shape architectural beauty (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).

## Living on the Lagoon: Acqua Alta, Subsidence, Sea-Level Rise and MOSE

The Venetian Lagoon is an enclosed bay of the Adriatic of about 550 square kilometres, and only around 8% of it is land (Wikipedia: Venetian Lagoon).
About 11% is open water or canals, and around 80% is mud flats, tidal shallows and salt marshes (Wikipedia: Venetian Lagoon).
It connects to the Adriatic through three inlets: Lido, Malamocco and Chioggia (Wikipedia: Venetian Lagoon).
Much of the present lagoon is the result of human intervention, and pumping of aquifers since the nineteenth century has increased subsidence (Wikipedia: Venetian Lagoon).

Acqua alta (Italian for "high water") is an exceptional tide peak that occurs periodically in the northern Adriatic, reaching its maximum in the Venetian Lagoon, where it partly floods Venice and Chioggia (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
It occurs mainly between autumn and spring, when astronomical tides are reinforced by winds that hamper the reflux: the sirocco, blowing north along the Adriatic, and the bora, whose effect is local to the lagoon's shape (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
The astronomical component can be predicted for years ahead, but the meteorological component can only be forecast for the very short run, and it is the main cause of emergencies that catch Venetians unprepared (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).

Supernormal tides are measured against a standard sea level defined by averaging the year 1897, and are categorised as follows (Wikipedia: Acqua alta):

- intense: 80 cm to 109 cm above the standard;
- very intense: 110 cm to 139 cm above the standard;
- exceptional: 140 cm or more above the standard.

The reference measurement is taken at the hydrographic station near the Basilica di Santa Maria della Salute (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
A city-commissioned study found that a tide up to 90 cm leaves Venice virtually unaffected, while 50 cm of additional water affects more than half the city (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
The highest level recorded was 194 cm on 4 November 1966 (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
On 13 November 2019 the tide peaked at 1.87 m, the highest since 1966, and more than 80% of the city was covered by water (Wikipedia: Venice).

Subsidence and eustasy (the rise of sea level) both add to the problem.
The city's Tide Monitoring and Forecast Centre estimates that Venice has lost 23 cm in elevation since 1897: 12 cm from natural causes and 13 cm from human-driven subsidence, with 2 cm regained through elastic recovery of the soil (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
Because of global warming, tides of 110 cm, which occur around four times a year, are expected to rise to 60 times a year within 50 years (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
Between 1993 and 2019, tide-gauge measurements corrected for subsidence show an average sea-level rise of about 2.76 ± 1.75 mm a year, above the 1.23 ± 0.13 mm a year estimated for 1872 to 2019 (Wikipedia: Sea level rise in Venice).
The likely rise by 2100 is 21 to 52 cm, within a wider range of projections (Wikipedia: Sea level rise in Venice).

MOSE (Modulo Sperimentale Elettromeccanico, "Experimental Electromechanical Module") is designed to protect Venice and the lagoon from tides of up to 3 metres (Wikipedia: MOSE).
It consists of 78 gates in four rows on the seabed at the three inlets, which can be raised to seal the lagoon from the Adriatic during high tide (Wikipedia: MOSE).
When a high tide is forecast, compressed air empties the gates so they rise on their hinges; raising takes about 30 minutes and lowering about 15, so the inlets are closed for an average of four to five hours (Wikipedia: MOSE).
The authorities set 110 cm as the optimum height for raising the gates, though they can be operated at any level (Wikipedia: MOSE).
As of 2023 the gates are raised for tides forecast above 1.30 m (Wikipedia: MOSE).
MOSE was first activated on 3 October 2020, protecting low-lying parts of the city, notably Piazza San Marco, from a predicted high tide (Wikipedia: MOSE).

Work began in 2003, and the project has suffered repeated delays, cost overruns and scandals (Wikipedia: MOSE).
It missed its 2018 and 2021 deadlines and is now set for completion in 2025 (Wikipedia: MOSE).
The estimated cost is €5.496 billion, €1.3 billion above initial projections (Wikipedia: MOSE), while another source says the cost has exceeded €6 billion (Wikipedia: Sea level rise in Venice).
In 2014, 35 people, including the mayor Giorgio Orsoni, were arrested on corruption charges connected with the project (Wikipedia: MOSE).

The criticisms should be presented fairly.
Opponents say the costs are much higher than alternatives used in the Netherlands and England, and that the integrated system is not "gradual, experimental and reversible" as the Special Law requires (Wikipedia: MOSE).
Critics also point to environmental effects on the lagoon's hydrogeological balance and ecosystem (Wikipedia: MOSE).
The engineer Luigi D'Alpaos argued that frequent raising would make the gates a "near-permanent wall", turning the lagoon into a "stagnant pool for algae and waste" (Wikipedia: MOSE).
Supporters say the system can handle rising waters, while others doubt it (Wikipedia: MOSE).
The sources do not settle this disagreement.

For visitors, the sources offer limited practical guidance.
Forecasts are issued by the city's Tide Monitoring and Forecast Centre, usually for the next 48 hours, and are announced through its website, phone lines, local newspapers, electronic displays and some vaporetto stops (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
Check comune.venezia.it for current forecasts and arrangements before you travel, because longer-range forecasts are less reliable (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
Low-lying areas can flood, and the wooden gangways on main paths are there to help pedestrians (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
The scale of future sea-level rise, and whether MOSE will be enough, remain open questions in the sources (Wikipedia: Sea level rise in Venice)(Wikipedia: MOSE).

## Venetian Painting and Architecture: Titian, Tintoretto, Veronese

Venetian painting was a major force in Italian Renaissance painting, and it gave primacy to colour over line, in contrast with the Mannerism prevalent elsewhere in Italy (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
It began with Giovanni Bellini and his brother Gentile Bellini and their workshops, and its major artists included Giorgione, Titian, Tintoretto, Paolo Veronese and Jacopo Bassano (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
Giorgione and Titian were both apprentices in Bellini's workshop, and Giorgione's idyllic Arcadian landscapes were taken up by Bellini and Titian (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
Venetian painters were among the first Italians to paint on canvas rather than wooden panels, and canvas was always available in a maritime power (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
Many of the great 16th-century masters were not natives of the city, coming instead from the mainland and the Republic's other territories (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).

### Decline and revival

The glories of the 16th century were followed by a great fall-off in the 17th, and an unexpected revival in the 18th, when Venetian painters enjoyed success around Europe (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
The most significant 17th-century artists working in Venice were immigrants, such as Domenico Fetti from Rome and Bernardo Strozzi from Genoa (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
In the 18th century Canaletto and Guardi specialised in views of the city and the lagoon, and Giovanni Battista Tiepolo is called the last great Venetian painter (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
The Venetian style effectively ended with the death of Guardi in 1793 and the extinction of the Republic in 1797 (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).

### Titian

Titian (c. 1488/1490 – 1576), born in Pieve di Cadore near Belluno, is the most important artist of Renaissance Venetian painting (Wikipedia: Titian).
Most modern scholars favour a birth date between 1488 and 1490, though Titian himself claimed in a letter to have been born in 1474, which seems most unlikely (Wikipedia: Titian).
His appointment as official painter to the Republic was delayed until 1523, and he became court painter to Charles V (Wikipedia: Titian) (Wikipedia: Titian).
His Assumption of the Virgin (1516) for the Frari is his largest single panel, and it created a sensation for its colour on a grand scale (Wikipedia: Titian).
Philip II received the mythological series known as the Poesie, inspired mostly by Ovid (Wikipedia: Titian).
His late works show open, loose brushwork, and his last painting, the Pietà, was meant for his own tomb in the Frari (Wikipedia: Titian) (Wikipedia: Titian).
Titian died on 27 August 1576 during the plague (Wikipedia: Titian).

### Tintoretto

Jacopo Robusti, known as Tintoretto (1518–1594), was born in Venice, the son of a dyer, and his nickname means 'little dyer' (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
His contemporaries both admired and criticised his speed and bold brushwork, and he was nicknamed il Furioso (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
His early biographers Carlo Ridolfi (1642) and Marco Boschini (1660) disagree on why Titian dismissed him after a few days, one blaming jealousy and the other a personality clash, so this account is disputed (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
In 1548 he received the commission for the Miracle of the Slave for the Scuola di S.
Marco, which established his reputation (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
Between 1565 and 1567, and again from 1575 to 1588, he painted for the Scuola Grande di San Rocco, and he won the first commission there by secretly installing a full-size painting on the ceiling (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
His Paradise for the Doge's Palace was completed largely by assistants, and modern critics rate it below his sketches (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
His style, with long strokes, muscular figures and dramatic gestures, is seen as a forerunner of the Baroque (Wikipedia: Tintoretto) (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).

### Veronese

Paolo Veronese (1528–1588) came from Verona, arrived in Venice in 1553, and was commissioned to paint huge fresco schemes for the Doge's Palace (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).

### Architectural traditions

Venetian architecture kept much of its Gothic form, and the Doge's Palace was rebuilt after fires but mostly behind its Gothic facades (Wikipedia: Venetian Renaissance).
St Mark's Basilica and the Piazza San Marco were a visible expression of the idea of Venice, and Byzantine styles continued to influence Renaissance art (Wikipedia: Venetian Renaissance).
Jacopo Sansovino designed the Biblioteca Marciana, which Palladio praised as probably the richest ever built (Wikipedia: Venetian Renaissance).

### What to look for in front of a Venetian painting

Look at colour first, since the sources describe colour as the school's defining aesthetic (Wikipedia: Venetian painting).
In Titian's later works, look at the loose brushwork, which the sources describe as open and sometimes dashed off with intense energy (Wikipedia: Titian).
In Tintoretto's works, look at how figures move through space, since the drama depends on gesture and foreshortening (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).

## Music and the Biennale

Venice was often called the "Republic of Music," and an anonymous 17th-century Frenchman is said to have remarked that "In every home, someone is playing a musical instrument or singing.
There is music everywhere." (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Relatively little is known about Venetian music before the 15th century, but the city's trade with East and West meant its music absorbed styles from many parts of Europe, North Africa and the Middle East. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Important chapters in instrumental music were written here: for lute and organ in the 16th century, for instrumental ensemble in the 17th, and for virtuoso performance in the 18th. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)

### Church music and the Gabrielis

Venice developed a distinctive church music tradition, with elaborate services at St Mark's Basilica. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Andrea and Giovanni Gabrieli wrote antiphonal brass music for the Basilica's acoustics, using double and triple choirs. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Their Sacrae Symphoniae (1597) and Canzoni (1608) are among the first published works of music. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)

### Opera and the first public theatres

Opera became a commercial enterprise in Venice in the 1630s, with the Teatro Tron, the first opera house opened to the public. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Opera theatres open to a fee-paying public date from 1637, after a long period of private performance before noble audiences. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Claudio Monteverdi, who moved from Mantua in 1613, rejuvenated the city's musical life. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
The opera season coincided with Carnevale, when Venice was an international meeting ground. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
Four orphanages of the Republic taught children trades, including music, and became music conservatories; Antonio Vivaldi taught at the Ospedale della Pietà. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)

### Composers named in the sources

- Claudio Monteverdi (1567–1643), composer, string player and choirmaster. (Wikipedia: Venice)
- Francesco Cavalli (1602–1676), baroque composer. (Wikipedia: Venice)
- Barbara Strozzi (1619–1677), composer and singer. (Wikipedia: Venice)
- Tomaso Albinoni (1671–1751), baroque composer. (Wikipedia: Venice)
- Antonio Vivaldi (1678–1741), composer and violinist of the Baroque era. (Wikipedia: Venice)

Many of Vivaldi's manuscripts are preserved on the island of San Giorgio, which is also an important site for classical concerts. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)
La Fenice, the best-known opera house, was built in 1786, suffered a disastrous fire in 1996 and was rebuilt. (Wikipedia: Music of Venice)

### The Venice Biennale

The Biennale has been held every other year since 1895, making it the oldest of its kind. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
The Venetian City Council resolved on 19 April 1893 to set up a biennial exhibition of Italian art. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
The first one opened on 30 April 1895 in the Giardini and was seen by 224,000 visitors. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
Its two main components, the Art Biennale and the Architecture Biennale, alternate years; since 2021 the Art Biennale has been held in even years. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
Other components include Biennale Musica (1930), the Venice Film Festival (1932) and Biennale Teatro (1934). (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
The main exhibition is held in Castello, in the Arsenale and the Giardini. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)

### Giardini pavilions and Collateral Events

The Giardini houses 30 permanent national pavilions, the first of which, Belgium's, was built in 1907. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
Countries without a Giardini pavilion exhibit in other venues across Venice. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
Galleries, museums and foundations stage independent exhibitions across the city, and some are chosen by the curator as Collateral Events. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)
Check the official Biennale site for the current edition's dates and opening arrangements before you go. (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale)

## The Essential Sights

The four sights in this section are the ones most visitors plan around first, and each rewards more than a quick look.
Planning allowances below are the guide's own estimates, not figures from the sources.
For current opening arrangements, ticket rules and the day-trip access fee, check veneziaunica.it and comune.venezia.it before you go.

### St Mark's Basilica

St Mark's matters because it was the state church of the Republic rather than a conventional cathedral.
Before 1797 it was the Doge's private chapel, and it became the patriarchal cathedral only in 1807 (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
Its design follows the sixth-century Church of the Holy Apostles in Constantinople, adapted to the site and to Venetian state ceremonies (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
The present structure, the third church on the site, was begun probably in 1063 (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
Many of its columns and reliefs are spoils from Constantinople after the Fourth Crusade, and the four bronze horses over the entry were among the plunder (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
The original horses are now kept inside; since 1974 copies have stood on the balcony over the central portal (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
The mosaics were added over centuries, and only about a third of them can be considered original after repeated restoration (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).

What to look for:
- The gold-ground mosaics in the domes and vaults, including the narthex cycle of Genesis and Exodus (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
- The Pala d'Oro, a Byzantine enamel altarpiece on gilded silver, installed in 1105 (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).
- The Gothic altar screen dated 1394 (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica).

Planning allowance (the guide's estimate): about one hour inside, plus time in the square.

### The Doge's Palace

The Doge's Palace housed the government of the Republic, the Doge's residence and a prison under one roof (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).
The Doge was the elected authority of the Republic (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).
The palace was first built in 810 and has been rebuilt repeatedly; its present form is Venetian Gothic (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).
The new Gothic palace began around 1340, and after fires in 1483, 1574 and 1577 the rebuilding respected the Gothic style despite neo-classical designs by Andrea Palladio (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).

What to look for:
- The Porta della Carta, the ceremonial gateway built in 1438–1442 (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).
- The Chamber of the Great Council, one of the largest rooms in Europe, with Tintoretto's Il Paradiso (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).
- The Bridge of Sighs, built shortly after 1600 to link the palace to the New Prisons (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).

The name "Bridge of Sighs" dates from the Romantic period, not from the bridge's construction (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).

Planning allowance (the guide's estimate): about one and a half to two hours.

### The Grand Canal

The Grand Canal is the largest channel in Venice and one of the city's main water-traffic corridors (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
It is 3.8 km long and 30 to 90 metres wide, making a large reverse-S shape through the central sestieri (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
More than 170 buildings line its banks, most dating from the 13th to the 18th century, and they show the wealth and art of the Republic (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
The sources read the rival palazzi as expressions of civic pride and of the deep bond with the lagoon (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
Notable palazzi include Ca' d'Oro, Ca' Rezzonico and Palazzo Venier dei Leoni, which houses the Peggy Guggenheim Collection (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
Most palaces rise straight from the water, so the best view of their fronts is from a boat (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).

Planning allowance (the guide's estimate): a vaporetto ride along the canal takes roughly thirty to forty minutes, with time for stops.

### The Rialto Bridge

The Rialto is the oldest of the four bridges across the Grand Canal, connecting San Marco and San Polo (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).
The first dry crossing was a pontoon bridge built in 1181, replaced in 1255 by a wooden bridge with a movable central section (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).
The timber bridge was damaged by fire in 1310, and collapsed in 1444 and again in 1524 (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).
In 1551 classical stone designs by Sansovino, Palladio and Vignola were judged unsuitable for the site (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).
The present single-span stone bridge by Antonio da Ponte was built between 1588 and 1591 (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).
Its engineering was so bold that Vincenzo Scamozzi predicted its ruin, yet it has become one of the city's icons (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).

Planning allowance (the guide's estimate): about thirty minutes to cross and look at the shops on the covered ramps.

## Less Visited Sights and Art Stops

Venice's famous monuments are only part of its art.
A few quieter stops let you see painting in the place it was made for.
The time allowances below are the guide's own planning estimates, not figures from any source.

### Scuola Grande di San Rocco

This is the best place to see Tintoretto at scale.
The building is the seat of a confraternity founded in 1478 and named after San Rocco, popularly regarded as a protector against plague (Wikipedia: Scuola Grande di San Rocco).
Tintoretto was commissioned in 1564, and the works are his or his assistants', including his son Domenico, executed between 1564 and 1587 (Wikipedia: Scuola Grande di San Rocco).
The lower hall concentrates on episodes from the life of the Virgin Mary, while the upper hall pairs Old Testament ceiling scenes with New Testament walls, so that together they show the biblical story from Fall to Redemption (Wikipedia: Scuola Grande di San Rocco).
Tintoretto won the commission through a bold trick: he secretly installed a full-sized painting on the ceiling and presented it as a gift on the day of the competition, an episode the sources call "the most notorious incident of Tintoretto's career" (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).
Planning estimate: allow one to two hours, since the paintings are dense and were made to be seen in a dusky half-light (Wikipedia: Tintoretto).

### Gallerie dell'Accademia

The Gallerie dell'Accademia is a museum of pre-19th-century art on the south bank of the Grand Canal, within the sestiere of Dorsoduro (Wikipedia: Gallerie dell'Accademia).
Its collection of Venetian painting up to the 19th century is generally arranged chronologically and includes Giorgione, Titian, Tintoretto and Veronese (Wikipedia: Gallerie dell'Accademia).
Leonardo da Vinci's The Vitruvian Man can only be exposed for a few weeks every six years for conservation reasons, so most visitors will not see it (Wikipedia: Gallerie dell'Accademia).
Planning estimate: allow one to two hours.

### Along the Grand Canal

The Grand Canal's banks are lined with more than 170 buildings, most dating from the 13th to the 18th century (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
- Ca' d'Oro: a 15th-century palace that still shows its original appearance (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
- Santa Maria della Salute: begun in 1631 by Baldassarre Longhena, a symbol of the Grand Canal (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
- Ca' Rezzonico: a Baroque palace with many carvings, begun by Longhena and later completed by Giorgio Massari (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
- Peggy Guggenheim Collection: housed in Palazzo Venier dei Leoni (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
Most palaces rise straight from the water, so their façades can only be seen from a boat (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).

### Biennale grounds in Castello

The formal Biennale is based in the Giardini park in Castello, which holds a large exhibition hall and national pavilions (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale).
The Giardini house 30 permanent national pavilions, the first built by Belgium in 1907 (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale).
The main exhibition also uses the halls of the Arsenale, and Collateral Events are staged across the city (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale).
Planning estimate: allow a half-day for the Giardini and Arsenale when the exhibition is open.

For current opening arrangements and access, check veneziaunica.it and comune.venezia.it before you go.

## The Sestieri and the Islands: Murano, Burano, Torcello

Venice's historic centre is divided into six districts, or sestieri, named Cannaregio, Castello, Dorsoduro, San Marco, San Polo and Santa Croce (Wikipedia: Venice).
The sources say little about each sestiere, so treat them mainly as the units by which the city is mapped and addressed.
Several sights in this guide sit in Dorsoduro, Castello and San Polo, including the Accademia and the Biennale grounds (Wikipedia: Gallerie dell'Accademia) (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale).
The mainland (terraferma) differs in character: most of the city's population lives there, in four boroughs: Mestre-Carpenedo, Marghera, Chirignago-Zelarino and Favaro Veneto (Wikipedia: Venice).
Mestre is the mainland's centre, linked to the historic city by the Ponte della Libertà road bridge (Wikipedia: Venice).

The lagoon islands make the best day trips from the historic city, and each has its own story.

### Murano

Murano is a series of islands linked by bridges about 1.5 km north of Venice, famous for glassmaking (Wikipedia: Murano).
Its glass tradition began when the Republic, fearing fire and the destruction of the city's mostly wooden buildings, ordered glassmakers to move their furnaces there in 1291 (Wikipedia: Murano).
Murano's glassmakers held a monopoly on high-quality glass for centuries, though the Republic partly lost it at the end of the sixteenth century (Wikipedia: Murano).
The Murano Glass Museum in the Palazzo Giustinian shows glass from Egyptian times to the present (Wikipedia: Murano).
Camaldolese hermits founded the Monastery of St Michael early in the second millennium; it became a centre of learning and printing (Wikipedia: Murano).
The cartographer Fra Mauro, whose maps were crucial to European exploration, was a monk of this community (Wikipedia: Murano).
French forces suppressed the monastery in 1810, and its grounds then became Venice's major cemetery (Wikipedia: Murano).
The church of Santa Maria e San Donato has a twelfth-century Byzantine mosaic pavement (Wikipedia: Murano).

### Burano

Burano, known for its lace and brightly coloured houses, is mainly a tourist economy (Wikipedia: Burano).
It lies 7 km from Venice, a 45-minute vaporetto trip from St Mark's Square (Wikipedia: Burano).
Lacemaking rose in the sixteenth century, declined in the eighteenth and revived after a lacemaking school opened in 1872 (Wikipedia: Burano).
Few now make lace by hand, as it is extremely time-consuming and therefore expensive (Wikipedia: Burano).
House colours follow a system: an owner who wants to repaint must ask the government, which names the permitted colours for that lot (Wikipedia: Burano).
A bridge links Burano to its neighbour Mazzorbo (Wikipedia: Burano).

### Torcello

Torcello is a sparsely populated island at the northern end of the lagoon, described as the parent island from which Venice was populated (Wikipedia: Torcello).
This parent-island claim is a traditional view that the sources present as a claim, not settled fact (Wikipedia: Torcello).
Refugees settled there after Attila destroyed Altinum in 452, according to the same account (Wikipedia: Torcello).
In 638 Torcello became the bishop's official seat for more than a thousand years, and the bishopric moved to Murano in 1689 (Wikipedia: Torcello).
Population estimates for its tenth-century peak range from 10,000 to 35,000, but recent archaeological estimates put it at no more than about 3,000 (Wikipedia: Torcello).
The Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta, founded in 639, is the oldest cathedral in the lagoon and has eleventh- and twelfth-century Byzantine mosaics (Wikipedia: Torcello).
The stone seat called Attila's Throne has nothing to do with the king of the Huns, according to the source (Wikipedia: Torcello).

## Getting Around, Food, Customs and Common Mistakes

Walking is the main way to see Venice, and the sources show why: the city is built on 126 islands separated by water and linked by 472 bridges (Wikipedia: Venice).
Some sources give different counts (118 islands and 438 bridges) (Wikipedia: Venice), so treat the exact numbers as approximate.
The maze of canals requires more than 400 bridges for foot traffic, and the Grand Canal has four bridges across it: the Rialto, the Accademia, the Scalzi and the Ponte della Costituzione, opened in 2011 (Wikipedia: Venice).
Because most traffic runs along the Grand Canal rather than across it, only one bridge crossed it until the 19th century (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).

Boats are the second way around.
The Azienda del Consorzio Trasporti Veneziano (ACTV) is the main public transport company, responsible for the vaporetti and motoscafi waterbus services (Wikipedia: Actv).
Vaporetti are high-capacity waterbuses that run regular routes along the Grand Canal and between the islands, while motoscafi are faster (Wikipedia: Actv).
The only gondole still in common use by Venetians are the traghetti, foot-passenger ferries that cross the Grand Canal where there are no convenient bridges; other gondolas are rented by tourists (Wikipedia: Venice).
ACTV is not responsible for the airport waterbus: routes between Marco Polo Airport and the lagoon are managed by Alilaguna (Wikipedia: Actv).
Alilaguna motor boats serve Venice, Lido and Murano from the airport (Wikipedia: Venice).
Bus connections with the airport are jointly run by ACTV and ATVO (Wikipedia: Actv).

The Grand Canal is a traffic corridor, not just a view.
It runs in a reverse-S shape from the Santa Lucia railway station to the basin at San Marco, and it is 3.8 km long (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).
Most palaces rise straight from the water without pavement, so you can see their fronts only from a boat (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).

Arrival points matter.
Piazzale Roma is one of the few places in Venice where buses and cars can enter (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)), and it is the main bus station (Wikipedia: Venice).
The Venice People Mover connects Tronchetto with Piazzale Roma, where visitors arrive by bus, taxi or car (Wikipedia: Venice).
The Venezia Santa Lucia railway station is a few steps from a vaporetti stop, and the Venezia Mestre station lies on the mainland; both are linked by the Ponte della Libertà (Wikipedia: Venice).

Venetian food is built on the lagoon.
Venetian cuisine is characterised by seafood, but Venice has no cuisine of its own; it blends local traditions with influences from distant contacts (Wikipedia: Venice).
Typical lagoon dishes include risi e bisi, a risotto with pancetta and peas; moleche, fried soft-shell crab; polenta e schie, small lagoon shrimp on soft polenta; and sarde in saor, fried sardines marinated in vinegar, onions and raisins (Wikipedia: Venetian cuisine).
Polenta is the common dish of the region, and bigoli, a fresh pasta, is typical (Wikipedia: Venetian cuisine).
Seppie al nero is cuttlefish cooked in its ink (Wikipedia: Venetian cuisine).
Cicheti, small tidbits similar to tapas, are also named (Wikipedia: Venice).
Among sweets, baicoli are butter-and-vanilla biscuits (Wikipedia: Venetian cuisine).

Customs and common mistakes, as the sources support them:
- Do not treat acqua alta as a rare event. It occurs mainly between autumn and spring, when winds reinforce the tides (Wikipedia: Acqua alta), and the city is often threatened by flood tides in those months (Wikipedia: Venice).
- Do not plan the islands as quick detours. Murano, Burano and Torcello each need their own boat time (see the island section).
- Do not treat legend as fact. The 25 March 421 founding date is a later tradition (Wikipedia: Venice), and the Venetian pride in its sea trade was not always matched by documentary proof (Wikipedia: Venice).
- Do not expect a Venetian cuisine that is separate from the region. Dishes such as tiramisu are not clearly Venetian in origin: one source says it was invented in Treviso (Wikipedia: Venetian cuisine).

For current fares, timetables, access rules and the day-trip access fee, check veneziaunica.it and comune.venezia.it before you go.

## Itineraries for One, Three and Five Days

These itineraries are the guide's own suggestions, not prescriptions from any source, and the pacing figures are planning estimates.
Check veneziaunica.it and comune.venezia.it for the day-trip access fee and current practical details before you go.
The water buses are run by ACTV, but the airport waterbus is run by Alilaguna, not ACTV, so plan that arrival separately (Wikipedia: Actv).

### One day: the essentials

Our reasoning: one day should cover the heart of the city and one long water ride.
Start at St Mark's Basilica, the former Doge's chapel beside the Doge's Palace, so the two form one morning group (Wikipedia: St Mark's Basilica) (Wikipedia: Doge's Palace).
Allow roughly two to three hours for both (estimate).
Walk to the Rialto Bridge, the oldest of the four bridges over the Grand Canal (Wikipedia: Rialto Bridge).
Finish with a Grand Canal vaporetto ride, since most palaces can be seen in full only from the water (Wikipedia: Grand Canal (Venice)).

### Three days: add depth

Day two adds the Gallerie dell'Accademia in Dorsoduro, with its masterpieces of Venetian painting up to the 19th century (Wikipedia: Gallerie dell'Accademia).
Day two also suits the Scuola Grande di San Rocco, whose paintings by Tintoretto and his workshop were made between 1564 and 1587 (Wikipedia: Scuola Grande di San Rocco).
Allow about two hours for the Accademia and ninety minutes for San Rocco (estimate).
Day three is a lagoon island: Murano for glass, or Burano for lace and coloured houses, by water bus (Wikipedia: Murano) (Wikipedia: Burano).

### Five days: slow down

Day four takes Burano and Torcello in one loop, since Burano is linked to Torcello by water bus (Wikipedia: Burano) (Wikipedia: Torcello).
Treat it as a half-day, because Burano is about 45 minutes by vaporetto from St Mark's (Wikipedia: Burano).
Day five suits Castello and the Biennale grounds when open, then slower neighbourhoods (Wikipedia: Venice Biennale).

### Weather and acqua alta fallbacks

Acqua alta, the high water that floods parts of the city, can disrupt walking routes, so keep indoor sights in reserve (Wikipedia: Acqua alta).
Swap an outdoor island visit for the Accademia, San Rocco or the Doge's Palace when the forecast is poor.
Keep a flexible day in each plan so you can move the island trip if the water rises.

Before you travel, check veneziaunica.it and comune.venezia.it for the day-trip access fee and current practical details.
