Why try to mechanise mathematics? The questions that frame the story

Interactive theorem proving means an arrangement where a machine and a human user work together to produce a formal proof1. At one extreme, the computer acts merely as a checker on a detailed formal proof produced by a human; at the other, the prover is highly automated while still subject to some degree of human guidance1. Automated theorem proving aims to prove assertions fully automatically, without human guidance1.

Automath was the first interactive system to use the Curry-Howard isomorphism to encode proofs1. The LCF tradition later turned each primitive inference rule into an ML function returning an object of type thm1. Later sections link these episodes to the design of today's proof assistants.

Logicism and Russell's paradox: why Principia needed types

Logicism is the view that some or all of mathematics can be reduced to formal logic, usually stated as a two-part thesis: all mathematical truths can be translated into logical truths, and all mathematical proofs can be recast as logical proofs2. Naive set theory lets any coherent condition determine a set: for any propositional function \(P(x)\) with \(x\) as a free variable, there exists a set whose members are exactly the things with property \(P\)3.

Let \(R\) be the set of all sets that are not members of themselves, and ask whether \(R \in R\)3. If \(R \in R\), then by definition \(R\) must not be a member of itself; if \(R \notin R\), then by definition \(R\) must be a member of itself3. The set of all teacups is not a member of itself, while the set of all non-teacups is a member of itself3. Burali-Forti had found a similar antinomy in 1897 about the set of ordinals, but Russell's paradox relies only on the primitive notion of set3. Because classical logic lets all sentences follow from a contradiction, many worried that no mathematical proof could be trusted if set theory was inconsistent3.

Russell's response was a theory of types, which arranges sentences into a hierarchy, with sentences about individuals at the bottom and sentences about sets of individuals above them3. The theory is motivated in large part by the vicious circle principle, which holds that no propositional function can be defined prior to specifying the function's scope of application3. The cost was high: the axiom of reducibility was introduced to overcome the not completely satisfactory effects of the type restriction, and many critics judged it too ad hoc2. As an analogy only, the hierarchy works like a type checker that rejects any expression applying a value to itself. Principia Mathematica, by Whitehead and Russell, appeared in three volumes in 1910, 1912 and 1913 as a defence of logicism, and it served as a major impetus for research in the foundations of mathematics throughout the twentieth century2.

Hilbert's programme: formalise everything and prove it consistent by finitary means

Formalising mathematics axiomatically means setting up a system of axioms and accepting as correct only those statements that can be derived from them by a finite number of logical steps4. Hilbert's programme, put forward in the early 1920s, asked for this formalisation of all of mathematics together with a proof that the axiomatisation is consistent5. A system is inconsistent if there is some statement A such that both A and ¬A are provable from the axioms4. Consistency mattered because a contradiction discovered by Zermelo and Russell had a catastrophic effect on mathematics4.

Hilbert separated finitary reasoning, which works with concrete signs surveyable in all their parts, from the ideal elements of classical mathematics5. The programme's goal was a finitary consistency proof of the formal system, showing that no formal derivation of a formula A and of its negation exists5. The companion hope was the Entscheidungsproblem, the search for a general mechanical process that decides whether a given formula is provable4.

Goals and outcomes must be kept apart. Gödel's work is generally taken to show that the programme cannot be carried out as originally conceived5. His second incompleteness theorem shows that a system such as Principia cannot prove its own consistency5. Turing and Church showed that the Entscheidungsproblem has no solution4. The sources report debate over whether the first or the second incompleteness theorem delivered the decisive blow, though those most directly involved judged that the theorems had a decisive impact5. After about 1934 it was almost universally accepted that the methods accepted as finitary before Gödel's results are formalisable in PA5.

Relativised programmes continue the idea: Gentzen published a consistency proof of first-order Peano Arithmetic in 1936, using transfinite induction up to epsilon-zero5. Reverse mathematics, developed by Friedman and Simpson, asks how much classical mathematics can be reduced to finitary mathematics, for instance showing that the Hahn-Banach theorem is provable in \(WKL_0\)5.

Gödel's incompleteness theorems: what formal systems cannot do

Gödel's two incompleteness theorems concern the limits of provability in formal axiomatic theories6. A formal system is a set of axioms with rules of inference whose axioms and proof relation are decidable, so a finite sequence of formulas can be mechanically checked as a proof6. The first theorem, in Rosser's improved form, says that any consistent formal system F in which a certain amount of elementary arithmetic can be carried out has statements that it can neither prove nor disprove6. The second says that such a system cannot prove its own consistency6.

The proof rests on coding: the symbols, formulas and proofs of F are each assigned a natural number, and the Gödel number of a formula A is written ⌈A⌉6. Because this coding is effective, syntactic operations become arithmetical functions, so neg(⌈A⌉) = ⌈¬A⌉6. In any consistent system containing Q, Robinson arithmetic, the strongly representable sets are exactly the recursive ones, where a recursive set is one that a mechanical procedure can decide6. The formula Prov_F(x) expresses that x is provable in F, and it is weakly representable6.

Because formulas are numbers, a formula can be built that talks about its own code, which is the idea of self-reference6. The diagonalization lemma mechanically constructs, for any formula A(x) with one free variable, a self-referential sentence D with F ⊢ D ↔ A(⌈D⌉)6. Applied to the negated provability predicate, the lemma yields the Gödel sentence G_F, which satisfies F ⊢ G_F ↔ ¬Prov_F(⌈G_F⌉)6. In informal explanations G_F is often said to say of itself that it is not provable, but such statements should be taken with a grain of salt6. If F proved G_F, it would prove ¬G_F, so F would be inconsistent; hence, if F is consistent, G_F is unprovable6.

Gödel's theorems pose, at least prima facie, serious problems for Hilbert's program6. Incompleteness concerns derivability in a particular system, not truth in an absolute sense6. It does not say that proofs cannot be checked mechanically, since the proof relation is decidable6. A proof assistant such as Lean checks proofs inside one fixed formal system, and no consistent system containing arithmetic proves every statement of its language6.

Decidability: Church, Turing and the limits of mechanical procedure

The Entscheidungsproblem asks whether there is a general mechanical procedure that decides, for any formula, whether it is provable in a calculus4. An effective method is a procedure set out in finitely many exact instructions that produces its result in finitely many steps and demands no insight, intuition or ingenuity from the person carrying it out4. A theory is decidable when its set of theorems is recursive, and by the Church-Turing thesis this is the same as there being a mechanical procedure that decides whether an arbitrary sentence is a theorem6.

Church identified effective calculability with recursiveness or with lambda-definability, meaning expressibility in Church's lambda-calculus, a notation for functions and their application, while Turing analysed human computation in terms of machines4. Both showed the Entscheidungsproblem for the functional calculus unsolvable, and Turing stated that the Hilbert Entscheidungsproblem can have no solution4.

Church's diagonal argument runs by contradiction from an effective enumeration \(A_1, A_2, \ldots\) of the formulas that have a normal form, meaning formulas containing no part that lambda-conversion can still reduce7. Church then defines \(E(n)\) to differ from \(A_n(n)\) on the diagonal, so \(E\) cannot be on the list, yet \(E\) is effectively calculable and so would have to appear on it, a contradiction7. Gödel's tools also give undecidability: any consistent theory containing Robinson arithmetic Q is undecidable6.

The proven result is that the Entscheidungsproblem has no solution relative to the formalised notions of recursiveness, lambda-definability and Turing computability, which coincide4. The Church-Turing thesis is different: it claims that these formal notions capture every effective method, and it is a thesis rather than a theorem4. Turing held that his arguments for the thesis are fundamentally appeals to intuition, and he described its status as something between a theorem and a definition4.

Turing noted that he saw no essential difference between a proof-checking machine and a proof-finding machine4. Checking a given proof is therefore a mechanical task, whereas finding a proof of an arbitrary provable formula has no general procedure, which is why proof assistants rely on human guidance.

Type theory and the Curry-Howard correspondence: proofs as programs

Section 5 asked what a machine can decide about provability, and this section asks how a proof can itself be a program that a machine checks. Propositions as Types has three levels: each proposition corresponds to a type, each proof to a program of that type, and each simplification of a proof to an evaluation of a program8. Gentzen's natural deduction gives each connective an introduction rule, which says how to assert it, and an elimination rule, which says how to use it8. Church's simply-typed lambda calculus ruled out self-application, a step similar to Russell's solution with types82.

The lambda calculus has only three constructs: variables, function abstraction and function application8. Here \(\lambda x.N\) is the function with parameter \(x\) and body \(N\), and \(M\,N\) applies a function \(M\) to an argument \(N\)8. The judgment \(M : A\) says that term \(M\) has type \(A\), where \(A \times B\) is the product type and \(A \to B\) the function type8.

Conjunction \(A \wedge B\) corresponds to the product type \(A \times B\), and a proof of \(A \wedge B\) consists of a proof of \(A\) and a proof of \(B\)8. Implication \(A \supset B\) corresponds to the function type \(A \to B\), so a proof of \(A \supset B\) turns a proof of \(A\) into a proof of \(B\)8. Abstracting over a variable \(z\) of type \(B \times A\) gives \(\lambda z.\langle \pi_2 z, \pi_1 z\rangle : (B \times A) \to (A \times B)\), which reads as a proof of \((B \wedge A) \supset (A \wedge B)\) and as a program that swaps the components of a pair8. Simplifying a proof corresponds to evaluating a program: a redex \((\lambda x.N)\,M\) is rewritten to the reduct \(N[M/x]\), and rewrites preserve types8.

Because each typing rule matches a proof rule, checking that a term has a given type is checking that the term is a proof of the corresponding proposition, so proof checking becomes type checking8. Dependent types let a type depend on a value, and Howard proposed that the quantifiers \(\forall\) and \(\exists\) correspond to such types8. The precise isomorphism is established for the fragments the source treats, such as simply typed lambda calculus and intuitionistic natural deduction8. The wider claim that the principle unifies logic, computation and other fields is the author's framing rather than a theorem8. The Church-Turing limits discussed earlier still apply to what a type checker can decide in general.

From Automath to Coq to Lean: how proof assistants grew out of these ideas

A proof assistant needs a small program that every proof must pass through before it is accepted, and this program is called the kernel9. The authors say that Lean has a relatively small trusted kernel, and that its rich API allows users to export their developments to other systems and implement their own reference checkers9. Tactics and automation are implemented in Lean itself, and users can modify and extend them9.

De Bruijn's Automath, conceived in the second half of the 1960s, was the earliest proof checker, designed so that a computer could check the correctness of a formalised text10. Its central insight was that types in a typed lambda calculus can be regarded as proof classes, with the elements of a type as proofs of a proposition10. A formalisation of Landau's Grundlagen der Analysis, about 500 pages of Automath text, was verified in October 1975 by a program only a few pages long10. The Automath project had a direct influence on the Calculus of Constructions, which became the basis of the proof assistant Coq10.

In the LCF approach, the primitive inference rules are the only constructors of an abstract type thm, which Milner introduced so that a guarantee holds that theorems were created by the primitive rules1. Coq was extended in the LCF style with tactics that operate on goals, in version 4.10 in 19891. Mizar, by contrast, keeps a batch-oriented style where proof scripts are checked in their entirety per run1.

Lean 4 is a reimplementation of the Lean theorem prover in Lean itself, which the authors say addresses many shortcomings of the previous versions9. It is both an extensible theorem prover and an efficient programming language, and its code generator produces C code, so users can write proof automation in Lean and load it as a plugin9. Users can modify and extend the parser, elaborator, tactics, decision procedures, pretty printer and code generator, and the system has a hygienic macro system custom-built for interactive theorem provers9.

Case study: the Four Colour Theorem checked in Coq

The Four Colour Theorem was first conjectured in 1852, and Appel and Haken proved it in 1976 with computer programs that carried out a case analysis too large to perform by hand11. Gonthier wrote a formal proof script covering both the mathematical and computational parts, and the Coq proof checker mechanically verified it11.

The key idea is computation inside the logic: in Coq, P(2 + 2) and P(4) are identical, so a proof of one serves as a proof of the other, which is called computational reflection11. The lemma check_reducible_valid says that if the boolean program check_reducible returns true then the proposition cfreducible holds, so a large case analysis is discharged by running a verified program11.

The rest of the stack is also trusted: the processor, its operating system, the Coq proof checker, and the OCaml compiler, but none of these is specific to the theorem, since they are off-the-shelf and tested on many other jobs11. The report demonstrates that a proof combining mathematics and large computation can be fully checked11. It does not show that proof assistants are generally easy to use.

Side by side: the main approaches compared

The table below compares the main approaches by the problem each addressed, the limitation it fixed, what it established, and its remaining cost.

  • Principia Mathematica (ramified type theory): addressed reducing mathematics to logic; restricted well-formed expressions by a theory of types, the mechanism used to avoid Russell's paradox; cost: many critics judged the axiom of reducibility too ad hoc2.
  • Hilbert's finitary formalism: addressed securing mathematics by formalisation and a finitary consistency proof; established only a programme, not a result; cost: Gödel's second theorem blocks a finitary consistency proof of Principia5.
  • Gödel's incompleteness theorems: addressed the limits of provability in consistent arithmetical systems; established that such systems have undecided sentences and cannot prove their own consistency; cost: the limit applies to every such system6.
  • Church's lambda-calculus and Turing machines: addressed the lack of a precise notion of effective procedure; established that the Entscheidungsproblem has no solution, while equating the formal notions with effectiveness is a thesis; cost: the general case of the Entscheidungsproblem is unsolvable4.
  • Curry-Howard type theory: addressed the gap between proof systems and computation; established a precise correspondence between propositions and types and between proofs and programs; cost: the correspondence applies to a range of logics8.
  • Automath: addressed mechanical checking of whole mathematical texts; established the earliest proof checker, which checks syntactic correctness with one fixed algorithm (a design choice)10.
  • Coq: addressed interactive proof on a type-theoretic foundation; established tactics that operate on goals in the LCF style, a design choice1.
  • Lean 4: addressed shortcomings of earlier Lean versions; established a reimplementation that is both prover and programming language, in the authors' description9.

Incompleteness and undecidability are fundamental limits, since they hold for any system of the stated kind64. Usability and automation are engineering limits1.

Summary and self-check questions

This tutorial asked whether all mathematical truth can be derived by rules, whether those rules can be shown consistent, and whether a machine can check the result. Russell's paradox forced types, and Principia Mathematica answered it with a hierarchy of types2. Gödel's second theorem says that for any consistent system F within which a certain amount of elementary arithmetic can be carried out, the consistency of F cannot be proved in F itself6. Church and Turing showed the Entscheidungsproblem unsolvable relative to their formal notions, and the claim that these capture every effective method is a thesis, not a theorem4. For each proof of a proposition there is a program of the corresponding type, and vice versa8. The Four Colour proof in Coq leaves the reader to check the short statement of the theorem and its definitions, while the remaining 60,000 lines are checked by Coq11.
